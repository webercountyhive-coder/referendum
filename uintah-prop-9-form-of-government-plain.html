What is Prop 9?

Today, three commissioners elected by the whole county make the laws and run the county. Prop 9 would change that.

Today vs. if Prop 9 passes

From the plan and the county's voter pamphlet.

TODAY: three commissioners make county law and run day-to-day operations.
IF IT PASSES: five council members, each elected from a district, make county law. A county manager hired by the council runs day-to-day operations.
Other offices, like the sheriff, clerk-auditor and attorney, stay elected either way.

If voters approve it, the first council election is in 2028 and the change takes effect Jan. 1, 2029.

How did it get on the ballot?

Five residents filed the plan. A petition needed 2,101 signatures, and the county counted 2,103. Signatures also had to come from at least three of the county's four areas, and three areas met their mark.

What can't the plan decide?

State law says a plan like this cannot set council members' pay or benefits, or say whether they are part-time. The council would decide those later.

What would it cost?

The county estimates about $560,000 a year. That number counts only the pay and benefits of a county manager and an assistant manager. It does not count any savings or any change in what elected officials cost.
